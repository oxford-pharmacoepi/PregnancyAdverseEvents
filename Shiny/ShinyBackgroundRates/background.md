## Background incidence rates of maternal adverse events and adverse events of special interest during pregnancy in four European countries: a multinational cohort study 
### Background
Surveillance of Adverse Events of Special Interest (AESI) supports vaccine and medicine safety monitoring, but pregnancy-specific background rates remain limited. Thereby, we estimated incidence rates of maternal adverse events (MAE) and AESI among pregnant populations using European healthcare data.
### Methods
We conducted a cohort study using nationwide registries from Norway and Sweden, primary care linked to hospital discharge data from Catalonia (Spain), and UK primary care data, all standardised to the OMOP common data model. Individuals with sex recorded as female, aged 12–55 years, and with pregnancies starting between January 1, 2018, and nine months before data extraction (December 2023 for Norway, August 2024 for Sweden, December 2024 for UK and Spain) were included. Incidence rates per 100 000 person-years were estimated for 13 MAE and 15 AESI, stratified by trimester, and maternal age. 
### Findings
We included 1 179 636 pregnancies (909 597 women; median maternal age 30-32 years). Miscarriage (10-16% of pregnancies) and dysfunctional labour (12-26%) were the most frequent MAE, followed by preterm delivery, gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM), antepartum haemorrhage (APH), and pre-eclampsia (0·3-8%). Severe outcomes like eclampsia, HELLP syndrome, and maternal death were rare (<0·1%). The most common AESI during pregnancy were deep vein thrombosis (DVT), pulmonary embolism (PE), immune thrombocytopenia (ITP), Bell’s palsy, anaphylaxis and myocarditis or pericarditis (0·01-0·1%). Miscarriage and ectopic pregnancy occurred mostly in the first trimester, whereas GDM, APH, hypertensive disorders, DVT, ITP, and Bell’s palsy peaked in the third trimester. GDM incidence increased with maternal age, while miscarriage and preterm delivery showed U-shaped distributions. 
### Interpretations
Rates varied by gestational trimester, maternal age, and data source. Compared with women of reproductive age, DVT, PE, and ITP were more frequent during pregnancy. These estimates provide reference values for pregnancy pharmacovigilance in Europe.
